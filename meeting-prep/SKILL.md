# Meeting Prep

Help the user walk into an important meeting knowing what they want, what others will push on, and exactly what to ask for. The brief is **private**. It's for the user only, never shared with attendees.

## Step 1: Check the meeting is worth it

If the goal is only to share information or get a simple yes/no, say so and offer to write an email instead. Meetings are worth it for discussing trade-offs, resolving disagreement, building a relationship, or unblocking something stuck.

## Step 2: Gather what you know

Ask for, or pull from what the user already shared:

- What the meeting is about, and when
- Who's attending and their roles
- What the user wants to come out of it
- Any history: past meetings, emails, disagreements

Don't hold things up with questions. If something is missing, make a reasonable guess and mark it **(guess)** so the user can correct it.

## Step 3: Write the brief

Keep it to one page. Use this format:

```markdown
# Prep: [Meeting name], [date]
Private: for [user] only

## What I want out of this
1. **Must get:** [the one thing that makes this a success]
2. **Should get:** [...]
3. **Nice to get:** [...]

## Background
[3-5 bullets: history, recent events, what's changed.]

## Who's in the room
| Person | What they care about | Likely view on this | How to approach them |
|--------|---------------------|---------------------|----------------------|
| [Name, role] | [...] | [supportive / neutral / skeptical] (guess) | [...] |

## What I'll say
1. [Key point, written the way I'd actually say it out loud]
2. [...]
3. [...]

## Questions they'll probably ask
| Question | My answer |
|----------|-----------|
| [...] | [...] |

## Risks
- [What could go wrong] → [what I'll do about it]

## My ask
[Specific person] to [specific thing] by [specific date].

## How I'll know it went well
- [Something I'll see or hear during the meeting, for example "They agree to a follow-up date"]
```

## Rules

- **The ask must be specific.** "Get alignment" is not an ask. "Ask Priya to approve the budget by Friday" is.
- **Rank the goals.** Force a choice about what matters most. Don't list five equal goals.
- **Write key points to be spoken.** Short sentences the user could say out loud, not bullet fragments.
- **At least three likely questions**, including the hardest one.
- **Mark guesses.** Anything inferred about people's views gets **(guess)**.
- Never invent facts about attendees. If you don't know, say so.

## After the meeting

Suggest using the `meeting-recap` skill on the notes afterwards to capture what was decided.
